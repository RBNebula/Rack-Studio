# Known limitations and remaining acceptance gates

This is a runnable, tested foundation. The complete simulator in the original brief has **not** passed final acceptance. See `acceptance.md` for the requirement matrix and `progress.md` for executed evidence.

## Engineering accuracy

- Lacing bars are generic parametric supports with a fixed 1U middle-hole mounting pattern. Default reach is 150 mm to the inside crossbar face; profile/tab dimensions and material/load compatibility are provisional. Collision uses solid rectangular members and conservative ear envelopes, including the visible screw openings. Ordinary surface waypoint lines do not follow a moved bar automatically.

- The [generic 24-port switch](generic-switch.md) models the supplied layout with 24 RJ45, two SFP cages and rear AC power, with editable provisional dimensions. Empty SFP cages are schematic assembly endpoints; actual modules/LC fibre interfaces, media/speed compatibility and module insertion are not modelled. Named Cisco/APC catalogue references remain separate incomplete drafts.

- Sliding rails now have per-instance geometry, bracket/post attachment, telescoping overlap and service-position checks. They are a provisional geometric template, with no inferred load rating or universal kit compatibility. Exact side keyholes, clips, latches, bearing mechanics and hardware animation remain open. Rail/cable interaction is geometric route validation and fixed-length checking, without tension/snags/slack physics. See [device-rails.md](device-rails.md).
- The new device designer and PCT110-inspired perforated tray are described in [model-designer.md](model-designer.md). Face components are schematic and may overlap; fans/grilles are decorative, and tray holes use a conservative closed collision floor. Tray movement/rotation, parent attachment and bound routing guides now exist; actual bracket/joint hardware and slot-aware passage remain open. The annotated rear PC photograph resolves the previously unknown video connector as DisplayPort; physical height and positions remain unmeasured.
- The photographed user's PC now has measured width/depth and confirmed 1U allocation, with two front and fifteen rear ports matched to manually rectified photographs. Physical height 43 mm, port centres/sizes, ears and fittings remain provisional; exact support hardware remains unknown; the later annotated photo identifies DisplayPort above HDMI. This one manually mapped model does not implement an interactive photo calibration tool. Its vent holes are visual details; routing uses conservative chassis/fitting envelopes and schematic connector markers. See [PC reference](user-pc-reference.md).
- User-confirmed conventions: screw-plane rack depth; ear-plane-to-rear device depth; U1 at the bottom; front/rear mounting; flat default; square holes with cage nuts; logical external destinations through exits; numeric, renameable cable labels; no default service loop;60mm termination allowance per end for bulk cable.
- Numerical rack frame dimensions, rear clearance and generic equipment remain illustrative. A regular19-inch mounting standard does not determine external beam geometry, enclosure width/depth, load capacity or vehicle fixings. Stepped construction, side-by-side rack middle beams and support metalwork still need dimensions.
- Named equipment is sourced as catalogue references with unresolved mechanical fields. Published maximum width may include ears; published depth does not establish the mounting-ear datum; rounded catalogue heights do not establish precision interference. Reference drafts with zero/unknown required dimensions cannot be saved as usable models.
- Port markers are not full connector/plug collision solids. Hole-centre markers do not cut actual rail holes or establish fastener/cage-nut dimensions. Mounting screw/rail compatibility is not verified from a generic kit name.
- Rack-mounted items now have explicit ear solids touching the exposed rail planes, including 1U/2U shelves. Their unknown plate thickness uses a labelled 2 mm preview; actual material thickness, front-fascia offsets and fastening hardware still need measured definitions. This geometric contact correction is tested independently of future installation animations.
- Frame envelopes, front/rear rail positions/profiles, shelf overall/usable dimensions and explicit support members are independently configurable. Current structural shapes are orthogonal solid boxes, angles and channels; arbitrary rotated shapes and hollow rectangular tubes remain future work. Generic frame previews and test examples are provisional. The requested reference file uses explicit construction with no inferred beams; actual supports and load capacity still need calibration. See [configurable-construction.md](configurable-construction.md).
- User photographs now guide flat frame/end covers and folded solid/perforated blanking plates. These are qualitative reference models with configurable example dimensions. Plate apertures and slots are real rendered holes; their collision proxy is conservatively closed. Rail holes remain centre marks rather than exact cage-nut apertures. Sheet gauge, bends, fasteners, material properties and load ratings are unverified; wire-mesh weave and perforated fold returns are not modelled. See [visual-references.md](visual-references.md).

## Routing and cable management

- Routing is deterministic geometric planning with final swept-envelope checks against supplied axis-aligned solids and oriented tray/fixing walls. It does not model gravity, flexible material mechanics, tension, installed excess slack, connector strain relief, electrical performance or every cable-to-cable interaction.
- Reused runs validate their shared offset and immediate reference clearance. General unrelated-cable/sibling breakout crossings remain outside a complete global cable dynamics/collision system. Clip/tie walls, tray lanes and a conservative bundle envelope are checked, with surface parent attachments. Separate cable identities and schedules are preserved, but this is not proof of a physically secured loom.
- Each route corner is a single circular fillet. When a cable must leave a connector at more than 90° (a rear-facing inlet to a front-facing outlet, for example), the connector lead keeps a 25 mm straight run (more if needed to clear its own chassis) and is lengthened along the connector direction, no further than the straight distance between the route ends, until the declared radius fits. Such hairpins can therefore project a lead beyond the rack face. Routes are not automatically re-planned as two 90° bends, and a radius that still does not fit is reported invalid. Whether a given explicit radius fits depends on device placement, and is not always monotonic in the radius (verification on 26 October 2026 found AP7922B placements valid at 35 mm but invalid at 28 and 50 mm). A valid route is not checked against the rack envelope, so a lengthened lead can pass beyond the rack frame without a warning.
- Unknown minimum bend specification yields an explicit draft-radius advisory. The engine's `valid` flag is a geometric result under supplied constraints, not a manufacturer's compliance statement. Unverified model values remain unverified in reports.
- Current exit points belong in clear openings. General enclosure panel Boolean openings, exit orientation/capacity restrictions, gland hardware and flexible brush bristles require further work.
- Wiring mode now supports actual surface clicks with a 10 mm normal offset, reusable point/two-click line guides, native port clicks and ordered/reversible cable links. Free guides stay fixed in world coordinates and can be dragged/rotated directly. Bound clip/tie/tray guides follow their physical parent. Shared bundles retain independent cable records and ordered breakout guides. Direct cable-segment dragging, flexible fixing dynamics and breakout fixing animations remain pending. See [modelling controls](modelling-workspace.md). See [Wiring mode](wiring-mode.md).

## Interaction, assets and reports

- Adjacent racks support independent axis-aligned layouts and origins, shared configurable members, cross-rack cables and portable assemblies. Automatic frame-overlap prevention for freely moved empty racks, rack rotation and structural load/vehicle fixing verification remain open. Assembly exports intentionally omit cross-rack/external connections and world-level shared members. Measurements are static world points, and hide/isolate affects only the view.
- Positioned vehicle endpoints include the external 3D route through the chosen exit and ignore stored manual external length to avoid double counting. Logical destinations retain the survey workflow. Full vehicle enclosure/opening geometry remains outside this milestone.

- Device drag, U snapping and rejection, support moves, stable cable connections, undo/redo, view controls and basic source/destination editing have browser coverage. The specified installation/removal/support/screw animation state machine is not implemented.
- Images persist as embedded PNG/JPEG/WebP and map to faces. Image crop, calibration, perspective correction, tracing, photograph-derived frames, extrusions and GLB/glTF import remain pending.
- CSV exports and authoritative calculations are implemented. XLSX/PDF reports, presentation-quality elevation exports, all table resize persistence/templates, complete library deletion UX and user-configurable connector-rule catalogues remain pending or partial. CSS table-column resizing is a temporary browser interaction and is not yet saved as a full schedule template.
- Stock is used for planning only. Cross-project stock reservation/commit, preterminated assembly stock quantities, pricing/supplier purchasing totals and user-managed alternative reel-size UI remain incomplete. The pure allocator already accepts alternative sizes, but that is not evidence of a complete stock-management interface.
- Local IndexedDB/JSON portability is implemented. Hosted accounts, secure shared libraries, access control, revisions, object storage and concurrency are future work; no private deployment is configured.

## Verification boundaries

- Browser interaction tests use Edge with software WebGL, not the user's physical GPU or measured rack. Actual screenshots are retained; a full controlled visual-regression baseline suite is still required.
- A synthetic42U/40-device/240-port/100-cable geometric benchmark is available. It is a smoke benchmark, with no constrained looms, imported geometry or complete browser FPS/memory/load instrumentation, and does not satisfy the full performance acceptance requirement.
- The complete section21 workflow, including rail/screw animations, physical cable tray fixing, looms/ties/pads, custom imported geometry and every requested export, has not succeeded. The project must remain marked incomplete until those systems and their regression checks are implemented and validated.
