# Wiring mode and shared guides

Open **3D Rack Designer → Wiring**. The mode has four tools:

1. **Connect ports**: click a physical source socket, then a compatible destination socket. Front/Rear camera views and zoom help with small connectors. Occupied/incompatible ports are rejected. IEC 60320 power couplers are gendered: a C13 outlet connects only to a C14 inlet and a C19 to a C20, so outlet-to-outlet and inlet-to-inlet cables are refused. A project saved before this rule that already holds such a cable still opens and can be edited; the power analysis reports it as `outlet-to-outlet`, and moving either of its ends must then pick a compatible port. The new cable is selected and receives the next available numeric label starting at 0. The inspector lets you rename it and enter its actual cable properties. Supplied length starts unset; diameter/clearance are provisional defaults and bend radius remains unknown until entered. Cat6A retains the configured 60 mm allowance at each end for bulk termination, visible separately in the schedule. No service loop is added.
2. **Waypoints**: click a visible rack, equipment, rail or tray surface. The actual intersected triangle and world-space normal place the cable centreline guide **10 mm outward from that surface**. A click in empty space does not invent a surface. **Shift + click** starts a line, then click a second surface position to finish it. Both ends receive their own 10 mm normal offset. **Esc** cancels an unfinished line or pending connection.
3. **Link cable**: choose a cable in the selector or select its rendered path, then click guide markers/labels in the required route order. A point contributes one routing constraint. A line contributes its entire straight A→B run. The route list can reverse a line to B→A, move a guide earlier/later, or unlink it. Multiple cables can link the same guide independently; geometry is referenced rather than copied.
4. **Select**: select cables/guides and use normal camera navigation. Right-drag pans and the wheel zooms in the other tools. Alt temporarily bypasses placement picking for camera navigation.

Expand **Reusable guides** to select a guide, rename it and edit its world coordinates in millimetres. A line exposes independent A/B positions. Moving a guide recalculates every linked cable. Unlink a guide from all cables before deleting it. Changes use project validation, undo/redo, IndexedDB save/reload and portable JSON. **Edit linked guides** in a cable inspector opens this workspace directly.

Free guides stay at their **world positions**, including when equipment slides forward. They are planning constraints, not physical clips. The 10 mm offset is applied at placement time; it does not bind a guide to the clicked object. Editing its coordinates can change that spacing. A cable's radius and configured clearance may require more than 10 mm, and a line between two individually valid surface points can pass through intervening hardware. Collision/bend checks still apply. A blocked line is reported invalid rather than accepted as a detour that silently misses the requested straight run.

Existing numeric and loom waypoints remain earlier constraints, followed by the ordered linked guides. A cable may also follow another cable's run; conflicting constraints require review. Free guides now support direct movement/rotation in **Modelling tools**. Surface-attached clip/tie hardware, bound tray lines and shared bundle guides follow their parent poses; cable-specific links provide later breakouts. See the [modelling guide](modelling-workspace.md). Direct cable-segment dragging and flexible cable physics remain open. Geometric path length does not establish signal performance or certify a cable product.

Import [wiring-guides.rack.json](../examples/wiring-guides.rack.json) for a two-PC reference with a shared point and line. See the [actual viewport capture](screenshots/wiring-guides.png), [numeric checks](validation/latest-checks.txt) and [browser workflow evidence](validation/wiring-browser-results.txt).

The rear sliding-rail correction places the far plate against the exposed rear face; the near plate bears on the inside of its post, leaving the outside clear for the PC ear. For front-mounted equipment with default 10 mm-thick posts at Z=0/800 mm, the near plate occupies Z=10…12 mm and the far plate Z=800…802 mm. These intervals meet the steel without entering it and mirror correctly for rear-mounted equipment. The near offset follows the independently editable post thickness. Hardware profile dimensions and mechanical compatibility remain provisional.
